# Memex

A hypothetical personal knowledge device conceived by Vannevar Bush in his 1945 essay *"As We May Think"*. The Memex would let a person store all their books, records, and communications in a compressed, indexed form, and traverse them via **associative trails** — links between documents that mirror the associative nature of human thought.

## Core idea

Bush's frustration was that human knowledge had grown beyond any individual's ability to navigate it. The Memex was his answer: a private, actively curated desk-sized device where a researcher could store everything they'd read, annotate it, and — crucially — build *trails* through the material that connected documents by association rather than rigid classification. Those trails could be shared with others.

## Relationship to the LLM Wiki

[[wiki/concepts/llm-wiki-pattern]] explicitly cites the Memex as the spiritual predecessor to the LLM Wiki pattern:

> "Bush's vision was closer to this than to what the web became: private, actively curated, with the connections between documents as valuable as the documents themselves."

The web generalized hyperlinks but abandoned the private, curated nature. The LLM Wiki pattern recaptures it. The gap Bush couldn't solve — **who does the maintenance** of building and keeping the associative trails current — is closed by the LLM.

## What Bush got right

- Knowledge is most useful when **pre-linked** rather than retrieved on demand
- The connections between documents are as valuable as the documents themselves
- Curation should be private and personal, not crowd-sourced

## What the LLM Wiki adds

- The LLM as tireless maintainer (Bush's missing piece)
- Explicit contradiction flagging across sources
- Structured synthesis pages, not just raw trails
